# Memorandum

**To:** Chief Executive Officer
**From:** Finance / Revenue Analytics
**Date:** 1 September 2026
**Subject:** The SDR expansion request — recommendation for the 15 September board meeting

---

## The answer

**Do not approve the $180k/month SDR expansion as tabled. Approve a $88.5k/month
Enterprise-focused pod instead, and fund it by switching off Paid Social.**

The sales machine is not broken — it is pointed at the wrong customers. Over the
last twelve months we spent $7.22m of sales and marketing to win 180 attributable
new logos worth $4.45m of new ARR. Eighty-six of those logos — 48% — were SMB.
They produced $580k: thirteen per cent of the value for nearly half the volume,
paying back in 62 months. The twenty Enterprise logos we won produced $1.76m and
pay back in twelve.

Doubling the SDR team pours another $180k a month into that same mix. Priced on
the productivity our own team actually delivered when it went from four reps to
nine, the incremental cost of acquisition would be **$79,788 per customer with a
28.0-month payback**. We have 18.2 months of cash. We cannot fund a 28-month
payback. The five-rep Enterprise pod costs half as much, has an incremental CAC
of **$45,671 and an 11.2-month payback**, and — because Enterprise deals are
3.5x larger — delivers *more* incremental ARR by month 18 than the full proposal
does.

| | Base | Sales proposal (+$180k/mo) | **Selective (recommended)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Incremental S&M | $0 | $180,000/mo | **$88,500/mo** |
| Paid Social retired | — | — | **−$75,500/mo** |
| Net incremental burn (month 6) | $0 | $186,987/mo | **$26,770/mo** |
| Incremental CAC | — | $79,788 | **$45,671** |
| CAC payback | — | 28.0 months | **11.2 months** |
| ARR at month 18 | $19.72m | $20.96m | **$21.04m** |
| Cash at month 18 | $806k | **−$1.70m** | **$1.07m** |
| Runway | 21 months | **14 months** | **23 months** |

The proposal as tabled runs the company out of cash in October 2027, one month
before the eighteen-month horizon the board uses. The selective option ends the
period with more ARR, more cash than doing nothing, and a longer runway.

## Where we actually stand

ARR closed August at **$12,374,314**, up **68.6%** year on year. Gross margin is
78.5%. Trailing-three-month net burn is **$335,925** — close enough to the $340k
you quoted — which is **18.2 months** of runway against $6.1m of cash. On the
forward model, where costs grow with revenue rather than staying frozen, the base
case has about 21 months.

Growth is real and it is bought, not inherited: of the $5.03m of net new ARR in
the last twelve months, $4.50m came from new logos and $1.73m from expansion,
against $1.23m of churn and contraction. I had expected to find that most of the
growth was product-led expansion in the installed base. It is not, and the
recommendation does not rest on that claim.

What the blended numbers hide is the mix. Net revenue retention of **104.7%**
looks respectable until it is split:

| Segment | Logos | ARR | NRR | GRR | % of ARR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | 52 | $4.85m | **121.1%** | 96.6% | 39.2% |
| Mid-Market | 204 | $5.71m | 104.4% | 92.9% | 46.1% |
| SMB | 291 | $1.81m | **82.0%** | 72.4% | 14.6% |

SMB is a fifth of the revenue, more than half the logos, and it leaks 18% of its
book every year. Enterprise NRR of 121% rests on only 33 anchor accounts, so
treat the precision, not the direction, with caution.

## The two things costing us money

**Paid Social.** It is our largest single programme line at **$75,500/month** —
larger than paid search — and it is the worst-performing channel we have. Over
twelve months it consumed $768k of programme spend and $1.11m of fully allocated
cost to buy **$241,428** of new ARR at an average deal size of $6,706. Its
customers churn at **6.34% per month**. CAC payback is **68.5 months** and
LTV/CAC is **0.29**. Every dollar we put into this channel returns 29 cents.

The cohort evidence points the same way and dates the problem. Six-month revenue
retention for Paid Social cohorts fell from 91.4% (2024 H2) to 64.8% (2025 H1)
and 66.1% (2025 H2), coinciding with the agency's move to broad-reach bidding in
November 2024. Cost per lead fell and volume tripled, which is why the channel
looked like it was working. In fairness, the small 2026 H1 cohort (n=6) recovered
to 98.8%; that is six accounts and does not outweigh a 68-month payback.

**Outbound sold into Mid-Market.** This is the finding that changes the shape of
the recommendation. Outbound as a whole pays back in 26.1 months, which reads as
mediocre. Split by segment, it is two different businesses:

| Outbound SDR, last 12 months | Logos | Avg ACV | CAC | Payback |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Enterprise | 13 | $93,486 | $70,164 | **12.3 months** |
| Mid-Market | 29 | $26,764 | $70,164 | **40.3 months** |
| SMB | 4 | $8,556 | $70,164 | 120 months |

An outbound deal costs roughly the same to win whatever its size. Twenty-nine of
our forty-six outbound wins went into the segment where that cost takes forty
months to come back. Under a harsher allocation that charges Enterprise four
times the sales effort of an SMB deal, Enterprise outbound still pays back in
18.8 months and stays the best slice.

For completeness, Partner is our strongest channel — 12.5-month payback, LTV/CAC
of 5.25 — but it produced only 16 logos on $19,800/month of spend and is capped
by partner-side capacity. It is worth more money; it is not worth $180k a month,
because it cannot absorb it.

## What SDR number 10 through 18 would actually produce

We have direct evidence, because we already ran this experiment. The SDR team
went from four heads to nine between January 2025 and September 2025. Meetings
per fully-ramped rep fell from **12.50 to 9.49**, a 24% decline, as territories
began to overlap. Fitting that decay as a power law and extending it to eighteen
reps gives **5.50 incremental meetings per added rep** — against the 12.5 a rep
delivered when the team was small.

At our observed outbound conversion (30.0% meeting-to-opportunity, 15.2%
opportunity-to-win) and the current outbound segment mix, nine more reps buy 2.26
wins a month. That is the $79,788 CAC and the 28.0-month payback. To clear an
18-month payback those reps would each need to book **8.57 meetings a month**;
the curve says they will book 5.50. The proposal is **36% short**, and no amount
of enablement closes a gap that size.

The pod is different for one reason only: it sells Enterprise. Same reps, same
conversion rates, 3.5x the deal size.

## How much of this is model choice

Little of it, deliberately. Five forecasting methods were run through
rolling-origin backtests under two window policies, 93 out-of-sample points each.
Holt's linear smoothing won on pooled MAPE (**3.44%**), ahead of the bottom-up
driver model (3.78%), drift (7.15%), log-linear (9.95%) and naive (13.15%). I
expected the driver model to win, since it is the only one that knows the series
is a subscription book; it did not, and the forecast uses Holt.

The conclusion does not depend on the forecaster. Every scenario uses the same
base trajectory, so the ranking is driven by cost and by conversion evidence, not
by the projection. The ARR bridge ties to $0.00 in all 36 months, the cohort
table foots to total MRR every month, and the Excel model reconciles to the SQL
warehouse on all 24 checks.

## My weakest assumption

**That the pod holds an Enterprise-weighted win mix.** Everything above turns on
the pod selling 58% Enterprise. It will not sell a single deal we have not
modelled at Enterprise economics, and sales teams drift down-market under quota
pressure — Mid-Market deals close faster and look the same on a leaderboard.

I tested it. The pod fails the 18-month payback test once its Enterprise share
falls below roughly **19%**. The current outbound team already runs at 28%, so
the pod only has to be no worse than the team we have. But if it drifts to
Mid-Market it becomes exactly the 40-month-payback motion we are trying to stop
funding — and the answer flips to "hire nobody."

The assumption I flagged first — how much the pod's territory overlaps the
existing team's list — turned out not to matter. Even at 100% overlap, where the
new reps add nothing but more people working the same accounts, payback is 16.4
months and still clears the bar. Segment mix is the one to watch, so it is the
one being made a condition below.
